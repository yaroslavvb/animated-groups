Worked example

Color group notation on a rosette

We analyze a three-colouring of a finite D6-rosette and identify the three groups entering Chaim Goodman-Strauss’s notation: the uncoloured symmetry group G, the stabilizer H of one chosen colour, and the subgroup K that fixes every colour class.

The group G Coset colouring The groups H and K Notation

1. The uncoloured rosette

Let P be an asymmetric motif in generic position. Let r denote counterclockwise rotation through 60°, and let s denote reflection in the horizontal axis. The orbit of P has twelve elements and realizes

G = ⟨r, s | r6 = e, s2 = e, srs = r−1⟩ ≅ D6, |G| = 12.

Thus G = {rk, srk : 0 ≤ k < 6}. We write operations on the right of P; consequently P · (sr) means “reflect, then rotate.” This convention agrees with the right-coset labels below.

As a finite rosette, the uncoloured pattern has orbifold signature ∗6•. The terminal bullet records the common fixed centre.

2. A three-colouring by right cosets

Choose the blue subgroup H = ⟨r3, s⟩ = {e, r3, s, sr3}. Its three right cosets partition G. Assigning one colour to each coset gives the displayed colouring.

Colour Right coset Elements
Blue H e, r3, s, sr3
Red Hr r, r4, sr, sr4
Green Hr2 r2, r5, sr2, sr5

Since [G : H] = 3, there are three colours. Only the reference coset H is itself a subgroup. In particular, Hr is the red colour class, not the stabilizer of red.

3. The single-colour stabilizer H and the colour-fixing subgroup K

The subgroup H stabilizes the blue class setwise. Its two reflections preserve blue while interchanging the other two colour classes. Hence H is larger than the subgroup that preserves all three colours separately.

One chosen colour

H = Stab(blue) = {e, r3, s, sr3},  H ≅ D2.

Every colour

K = {e, r3} = ⟨r3⟩,  K ≅ C2.

The red and green stabilizers are the conjugates Hred = r−1Hr and Hgreen = r−2Hr2. Their common intersection is K = H ∩ r−1Hr ∩ r−2Hr2. In orbifold notation, H has signature ∗2• and K has signature 2•.

4. Chaim’s G3/H/K notation

Group Order Role Rosette signature
G 12 All uncoloured symmetries ∗6•
H 4 Stabilizer of one colour ∗2•
K 2 Fixes every colour class 2•

The full description is

(∗6•)3/(∗2•)/(2•)

Since H ≠ K, Chaim suppresses H and uses a double slash:

(∗6•)3//(2•)

The superscript 3 is the number of colours, equivalently [G : H]. It is not [G : K], which equals 6 in this example. The induced behaviour of the generators on the colour names is deliberately deferred.

Example. The R-rosette is adapted from Dad’s original diagram, with a uniform r, s convention and finite-rosette signatures.

Reference. J. H. Conway, H. Burgiel, and C. Goodman-Strauss, The Symmetries of Things, p. 155 for the Gp/H/K convention.
