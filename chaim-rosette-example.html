Worked example · finite colour symmetry

Color group notation on a rosette

Start with one asymmetric letter R, make all twelve copies allowed by the symmetries of a regular hexagon, and add three colours. From that single picture we will identify \(G\), the single-colour stabilizer \(H\), and the subgroup \(K\) that leaves every colour unchanged.

This first lesson stays with the operations visible in the rosette. How individual generators permute the colour names comes later.

Destination

\[(*6\mathord\bullet)^3//(2\mathord\bullet)\]

Three colours, a finite sixfold kaleidoscopic rosette, and a twofold subgroup that fixes every colour.

  1. 01

    Begin without colour

    Twelve operations make the rosette

    Place an asymmetric motif \(P\) away from the centre. Let \(r\) be a counterclockwise turn through \(60^\circ\), and let \(s\) be reflection in the horizontal axis. Rotations and reflections generate twelve distinct copies of \(P\).

    \[ G=\langle r,s\mid r^6=e,\ s^2=e,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle \cong D_6,\qquad |G|=12. \]

    We use the convention that \(D_6\) has twelve elements: six rotations \(e,r,\ldots,r^5\) and six reflections \(s,sr,\ldots,sr^5\).

    Reading the labels. To match the right-coset drawing, operations act on the motif from the right: \(P\mathbin{\cdot}(sr)=(P\mathbin{\cdot}s)\mathbin{\cdot}r\). Thus the label \(sr\) says “reflect, then rotate.”

    \(*6\mathord\bullet\) Rosette signature for \(G\). The star records mirrors, \(6\) records the sixfold centre, and the bullet says every operation fixes that centre.

  2. 02

    Partition the operations

    Three right cosets supply three colours

    Choose the four operations shown in blue as the reference subgroup

    \[H=\{e,r^3,s,sr^3\}.\]

    The right cosets of \(H\) partition the twelve operations into three disjoint blocks of four. We use one colour for each block.

    Blue \(H=\{e,r^3,s,sr^3\}\)
    Red \(Hr=\{r,r^4,sr,sr^4\}\)
    Green \(Hr^2=\{r^2,r^5,sr^2,sr^5\}\)

    Since \([G:H]=12/4=3\), there are exactly three cosets and therefore three colours.

    Coset is not stabilizer

    The blue block \(H\) is also the blue stabilizer because blue was chosen as the reference colour. But \(Hr\) is not the red stabilizer: it is a coset, does not contain \(e\), and is not a subgroup. The red and green stabilizers are conjugate copies of \(H\).

  3. 03

    Fix all three colour classes

    Only two operations leave every colour unchanged

    “Leave colours unchanged” means preserve each colour class as a set. An operation may move individual letters, but every blue letter must land on a blue letter, every red letter on red, and every green letter on green.

    For this rosette the complete list is

    \[K=\{e,r^3\}=\langle r^3\rangle\cong C_2.\]

    The identity changes nothing. The half-turn \(r^3\) exchanges opposite copies, which have the same colour. No reflection and no \(60^\circ\) or \(120^\circ\) turn preserves all three colour classes.

    \(2\mathord\bullet\) Rosette signature for \(K\). It contains one twofold rotation about the fixed centre and no mirrors.

  4. 04

    Keep one chosen colour

    The single-colour stabilizer is larger than \(K\)

    The subgroup \(H\) preserves the blue class, but it does not have to preserve red and green separately:

    \[H=\operatorname{Stab}(\text{blue})=\{e,r^3,s,sr^3\}.\]

    Besides \(e\) and the half-turn, \(H\) contains reflections in two perpendicular axes. Those reflections keep the blue set blue even when they exchange the other two colour classes. Hence \(K\subsetneq H\).

    Red and green have their own stabilizers. They are rotated, conjugate copies of \(H\), so Chaim’s notation records the type of one chosen colour stabilizer rather than listing all three.

    \(*2\mathord\bullet\) Rosette signature for \(H\). Two perpendicular mirrors meet at a twofold fixed centre.

  5. 05

    Assemble the notation

    Read the rosette as \(G^3/H/K\)

    G

    \(*6\mathord\bullet\)

    All twelve geometric symmetries of the uncoloured rosette.

    H

    \(*2\mathord\bullet\)

    The four operations preserving one chosen colour.

    K

    \(2\mathord\bullet\)

    The two operations preserving every colour separately.

    Full form

    \[(*6\mathord\bullet)^3/(*2\mathord\bullet)/(2\mathord\bullet)\]

    →

    Chaim’s abbreviated form

    \[(*6\mathord\bullet)^3//(2\mathord\bullet)\]

    The superscript \(3\) counts the colours. The double slash says \(H\ne K\): preserving one colour is less restrictive than preserving all three. Chaim suppresses \(H\) in this case, but the missing single-colour stabilizer is still part of the construction.
