A reading guide · geometry, time, and colour

Three notations share the same symbols

A row in the correspondence atlas may show a spatial orbifold, a clockwork symbol, a short colour signature, and a full colour type. They describe related data, but a subscript or superscript means something different in each layer.

The rule that prevents most mistakes

In a short colour signature, a superscript labels a generator slot—not whichever printed character happens to be nearest. Rotation labels are placed before cone digits; mirror labels occupy the slots after * or a preceding corner; crosscap and translation labels follow their own symbols.

01

Keep the four displays separate

The atlas puts four compact descriptions next to one another. Read the column or label first, then interpret its decorations.

Spatial orbifold

3*3

Topology and spatial isometries only: rotations, mirrors, glides, and translations.

Clockwork symbol

r32*~3

Spatial operations decorated by offsets in one period of forward time.

Short colour signature

33*23

Orders of the colour permutations induced by selected spatial generators.

Full colour type

3*36/333

Parent group, number of colours, and the subgroup fixing every colour.

Subscript = time fraction. Local superscript = permutation order. Global exponent = number of colours.

02

Conway orbifold symbols describe space

First erase all clockwork decorations and colour superscripts. What remains is Conway’s orbifold symbol for the projected wallpaper group.

n

Cone point. A free rotation of spatial order n. Thus 3 means a 120° gyration point.

*

Mirror boundary begins. Digits after the star are corner orders on that boundary; they arise from adjacent reflections.

×

Crosscap. In a plane group it records glide-reflection structure, not a mirror line.

◦

Handle or wonder ring. For wallpaper groups it is the translation-only torus, Hermann–Mauguin p1.

03

Clockwork decorations describe time

The forward catalog decorates the spatial symbol by a phase in ℝ/ℤ, where one unit is one complete film period. These marks are catalog notation rather than standard Conway notation.

nk
The spatial n-fold rotation advances the clock by k/n of a period. The omitted subscript means zero. Example: 32 pairs a 120° rotation with a 2/3-period advance.
~
A tilde marks a mirror arc whose reflection is paired with a half-period advance—a time glide. Its position follows the mirror arc, as in *~3 or ~*×½.
×q
A spatial glide or crosscap generator with clock offset q. Here the catalog prints the fraction itself, for example ×½ or ×¼.
c · r
Spacetime-lattice setting prefixes. In this catalog c marks a centered half-cell/half-period setting and r a one-third-stacked setting. They are not extra orbifold features.
/1′ · /m′
A separate primed clause, where present, records the time-reversing coset. Do not confuse time reversal with the forward phase advances represented by subscripts and tildes. The 51-row correspondence atlas uses forward-time groups only.

Same numeral, different question

In 32, the 2 answers “what fraction of a period?” In *2, the 2 answers “what is the order of this generator’s colour permutation?”

04

Short colour signatures label generator slots

In the Conway–Burgiel–Goodman-Strauss convention, replace each displayed generator’s colour permutation by its order. Order 1 fixes every colour; order 2 is an involution; and so on.

Free rotations

αA βB …

The superscript is placed before the cone digit.

Mirror boundary

*PaQbRc …

P, Q, R label successive reflection generators. Their slots occur after the star or after the preceding corner digit. They do not label those corner digits.

Crosscap / glide

×Z

The superscript follows the crosscap and labels its glide generator.

Handle / translations

◦X,Y

The two labels follow the ring and belong to a chosen translation pair.

33*23

33*23

The first 3 labels the free 3-fold rotation. The 2 after the star labels a mirror generator. The last 3 is a spatial corner order and carries no colour label.

*2×2

*2×2

The mirror generator and the glide generator each swap colours. Both superscripts print after a symbol, but each belongs to a different generator slot.

663322

663322

Three free rotations act on the colours with orders 6, 3, and 2. Here every superscript is placed before its cone digit.

There is no universal “the superscript belongs to the symbol on its left” or “on its right” rule. Parse the orbifold presentation into generator slots first.

05

Full colour types carry the invariant subgroup data

Gp/H/K

G
The full uncoloured symmetry group.
p
The number of colour classes in the transitive colour action.
H
The subgroup that preserves one selected colour.
K
The kernel: the normal subgroup that fixes every colour.

When H = K, the book abbreviates the type to Gp/K. Otherwise it may omit H with a double slash, Gp//K. The atlas’s regular cyclic actions have H = K, so they use one slash.

Local generator labels

*2×2

Each 2 is a permutation order.

≠

Global colour type

*×2/◦

The exponent 2 counts colours.

The book normally omits the global exponent 2 in a twofold chapter, printing *×/◦. Parentheses around the full parent group— (*×)2/◦—are an explanatory aid on this page, not an additional operation.

A short signature can depend on a generator choice and records only permutation orders. For three or more colours it can lose information. The full Gp/H/K type is the safer identifier.

06

The phase-to-colour dictionary

Normalize one film period to 1. If all forward phase offsets have denominators dividing N, label the colours by ℤN. A spatial operation with phase j/N acts by

colour k ↦ k + j  (mod N).

This produces a phase character κ: G → CN. Its kernel K = ker κ consists of the spatial operations that have zero phase and therefore fix every colour. In the regular cases displayed by the atlas, G/K ≅ CN and [G:K] = N.

Reduce the phase fraction before reading the short signature: a phase a/b in lowest terms induces a colour permutation of order b. The numerator tells which power of that cycle occurs; the short signature forgets that direction.

07

Worked examples

Wallpaper cm · one mirror and one glide

~*×½ ↔ *2×2 ↔ *×/◦

Open g9 in the atlas
  1. Erase time marks: *×, the orbifold for cm.
  2. The tilde gives the mirror reflection phase 1/2; ×½ gives the glide phase 1/2.
  3. Each acts as a transposition on two colours, yielding *2×2.
  4. The zero-phase kernel is ◦, so the book prints the twofold type *×/◦.

g235 · the mixed two-by-three case

Read the clockwork symbol from left to right. The r setting supplies one-third stacking. The generator 32 is a 120° rotation with phase 2/3. The star begins a mirror boundary, the tilde makes its reflection a half-period time glide, and the final 3 is the spatial corner order.

In colour language, the chosen 3-fold rotation advances six-colour labels by four steps and has permutation order 3; the time-glide reflection advances them by three steps and has order 2. The combined phase image has order lcm(3,2) = 6. That is why the short form is 33*23, while the full type has a global exponent 6.

The name g235 is local to the forward catalog. The spatial groups p31m and p3 are standard. The composite sixfold type is checked through the index-2 layer 3*3/333 and index-3 layer 3333/333; it is not a numbered crystallographic group named “g235.”

08

Gotchas worth checking every time

  1. Do not attach a superscript by proximity. In *23, the 2 labels a reflection generator; the 3 is the corner order formed by reflections.
  2. Do not mix local and global superscripts. The 2s in *2×2 are generator permutation orders. The 6 in 3*36/333 is the number of colours.
  3. A corner digit is not an independent free rotation. Digits to the right of * encode products of adjacent reflections; digits before the star are free cone points.
  4. The short signature is not always an invariant identifier. Equivalent generator choices can produce several signatures, and permutations with the same order can still differ. Prefer the full colour type and kernel.
  5. Parenthesized numbers in the book tables are counts. A separate (2) or (6) in the “Number” column counts equivalent signatures or cases. It is neither a colour count nor a generator’s permutation order.
  6. CN is the phase/colour quotient here. It is not automatically the spatial point group. A group with parent 3*3 can have colour quotient C6.
  7. Catalog IDs are addresses, not standard names. Labels such as g235 identify rows in this project’s catalog and should be quoted with their symbol or parent group elsewhere.

09

Status, gallery notation, and sources

The undecorated orbifold symbols and the colour-group conventions come from J. H. Conway, H. Burgiel, and C. Goodman-Strauss, The Symmetries of Things. The clockwork subscripts, tildes, and lattice prefixes are the notation used by this project’s forward catalog; they are not a standard extension of Conway notation.

The visualization gallery also uses an explicit construction label such as Q⟦g ↦ τ1/2⟧. Here Q is the spatial quotient, g names a chosen spatial generator, and τa is phase translation by a. A symbol ιb means phase reversal followed by a shift by b. This double-bracket syntax is an explicit, project-specific generator map; it is separate from both the forward-catalog clockwork notation and the book’s colour notation.
